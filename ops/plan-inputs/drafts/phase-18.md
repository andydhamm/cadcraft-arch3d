# Phase 18 plan: Interior finish, kitchen and bath

Phase 18 (interior finish, kitchen and bath) in 20 packages. Two groundwork packages run first and in sequence: p18-observables (the whole phase-18 data model in doc, the arch counter-outline module with the corner and face stubs, cabinet option hooks, the check lib and the two carry-forward checks) and p18-scene-plumbing (new surfaces, part and owner-kind mapping, four new incremental sections, the wall/niche/cabinet build hooks with one stub file per owner). Then 14 feature packages fill only their own files: room-type finishes (new Finish variants and the room-type template), the molding kit (Rust profiles, the Moldings library folder, polyline profiles via library.add, the sweep), room moldings (base/chair/crown runs and the brief's numbers), crown wrapping soffits and trays (after phase 16), molding lines, cabinet crown and light rail, countertops (detach, from scratch, edge moves, area), countertop shapes (arcs, radius grip, edge profiles, waterfall), backsplashes, cabinet construction and face layouts (door pairs above 0.686 m), face appliances, corner cabinets with one counter round the corner, fillers and open shelves, glass and pony walls, shower doors and niches. Three closing packages: exports (IFC coverings, glass material, countertops/backsplashes in their cabinets' IfcFurniture, glTF/OBJ names), the incremental rebuild (scene_cache_equals_full) and the demo (docs/arch3d/showcase/phase-18-demo.json). Checks 01-20 in tests/acceptance/phase-18.

CODE FACTS THAT SHAPED THE PLAN (checked on op/next 576538153):
- Automatic counters are derived, not stored: casework_layout::counter_runs groups in-line touching base cabinets (one CounterRun per straight run, a lone cabinet is a run of one) and arch3d builds one `counter` mesh per run in its owner's part. Custom countertops therefore are a new object (ct<n>) that claims its cabinets; counter_runs skips claimed cabinets.
- The legacy front layout (`casework_layout::fronts`) spans v from toe to height - toe (0.1..0.776 on a 0.876 base cabinet) and has no vertical reveal between drawers; phase-13/14 unit tests pin those numbers. Explicit face layouts (new) use the real face toe..height with 3 mm reveals between rows; cabinets without one keep the legacy layout byte for byte.
- Door pairs: `cabinet_defaults` (SINGLE_DOOR_MAX_WIDTH 0.6) and the cabinet command (`doors.unwrap_or(if w > 0.6 {2} else {1})`) pair above 0.6 m; the brief replaces this with 0.686 m. No acceptance check uses a width in (0.6, 0.686].
- The door/window commands set `casing_width` to their `casing` param, default 0 (Opening::new's 0.05 is overwritten), and arch3d builds the casing only on the wall's RIGHT face (the band offset lies at -normal). So a 0.9 m door from `door {...}` has a trim width of 0.96 (0.9 + frame 0.06) on both faces; with `"casing": 0.05` it is 1.06 on the right face and 0.96 on the left. The check reads the value from arch.inspect, as the brief says.
- Room types do not survive `roomdetect` (it replaces rooms; only floor_finish is inherited). Phase-18 room moldings are inherited the same way. Room-type finishes are therefore derived from the type at every read (no stored copy), which also makes "takes them when detected or its type changes" true by construction; a painter edit (model.paints) always wins; a type change clears the room's phase-13 floor_finish.
- Finish has 4 variants matched exhaustively in arch3d texture.rs, io glb.rs (roughness), render3d pipeline.rs (roughness); render3d textures.rs sizes its array as 1 + Finish::ALL.len() layers plus MAX_SCENE_LAYERS = 251, which would pass WebGL2's 256 with 5 more finishes: p18-room-finishes sets MAX_SCENE_LAYERS = 256 - LAYERS. Phase 19 already tolerates new Finish variants.
- Every `wall-<x>` material is treated as a finished exterior face by paint::apply and the GLB/IFC namers, and every material containing `glass` maps to the Opening kind in ObjectKind::from_part/ArchKind::from_part. So glass walls use material `glass-wall` with an explicit material-aware owner mapping (incremental::object_kind_of, probe::arch_kind), and niche backs use `niche-back:o<n>` (owner: the wall), never `wall-niche`.
- Materials starting with `glass` render transparent (render3d material_pbr alpha 0.35): glass walls and shower-door glass get that for free.
- There is no wall.edit command; walls are created by `wall` and stretched by `arch.wall.stretch`. Pony walls get their own `wall.split` command.
- arch.inspect rows for cabinets are extended after inspect_casework by helpers (inspect_cabinet_parts); every phase-18 package adds its own helper the same way.
- library.add takes arch labels (f, cb, fx); a molding profile from a drawn polyline needs `"kind": "molding"` and an entity handle (from `pline {"vertices", "closed": true}` -> {"handle"}).
- Arch handle ranges are 0x8000 wide; IFC has no IfcMaterial yet (p18-exports adds IfcMaterial + IfcRelAssociatesMaterial for glass walls only).
- (Re-checked on op/next 7f78b7024 in review round 1.) A build3d probe answers the FIRST thing that contains the point: furnishing boxes, then electrical boxes, then meshes in scene order (arch3d probe.rs `probe`). Cabinets come before the four new phase-18 sections, and a counter-hosted sink's furnishing box spans its counter's whole thickness over the cut-out (library/place.rs: elevation = top - size_z + 0.01, so 0.724..0.924 for own:sink-kitchen). Checks therefore probe a countertop or a waterfall only where no cabinet carcass, front or furnishing box stands, and test cut-outs on geometry (GLB coverage, as phase-14 check 05 does), never with a probe.
- (Checked in review round 2.) Every room ceiling is a 20 mm `ceiling` solid from c - 0.02 to c (floors::ceiling_surface; phase 16's arch3d ceiling/stack.rs `slabs` under every rough piece, a tray's raised piece included), and phase 16 builds a vertical tray's side (in a flat-ceiling room) as a `ceiling` band over the 0.02 m strip just inside the tray outline, from (the ceiling around it - 0.02) to (tray top - 0.02) (p16-trays side_meshes, SIDE = 0.02). The rooms section comes before the four phase-18 sections and mesh positions are f32 (f32(2.58) = 2.5799999 < 2.58), so a molding probe within 0.02 below a finished ceiling, or inside a tray's side strip, answers `ceiling`: checks probe crowns at least 0.02 below the ceiling (check 03 at 2.35 under 2.4, check 04 at 2.35 under 2.4 and 2.55 under the tray's 2.6).
- (Checked in review round 2.) counter_runs makes the cabinet with the lowest u along a run its owner (`cur.first()`, crates/arch/src/casework_layout.rs), and only the owner builds the run's counter mesh (`run.owner == c.id`, crates/arch3d/src/casework.rs): a filler at the start of a run owns it, so the filler branch of the cabinet hook must still build the counter.
- arch3d walls.rs cuts every opening at (width + ROUGH_ALLOWANCE) x (sill .. sill + height + ROUGH_ALLOWANCE) in opening_holes and bottom_door_notches and ignores Opening.rough_*; place_opening overwrites rough_* with size + 0.12. Frameless openings (niches, shower doors) need their own allowance: `cadcraft_arch::opening::cut_allowance` (p18-shower-niche).
- engine library.rs `for_query` parses the `for` key as a Surface (room/<r>/floor|ceiling, cabinet/<c>/carcass|front|counter|pull, ...) and checks that it exists BEFORE fits::query_for, so a `for` target that is not a surface needs its own branch there (p18-molding-kit).
- validate_cabinet ties fronts to counts (Doors needs doors >= 1, DrawerOverDoor doors >= 1 and drawers >= 1). ArchModel::is_empty lists every collection (phase 16 added trays, soffits, foundations) and gates the DXF arch payload (io dxf_arch.rs arch_chunks) and metric units (Drawing::touch_arch).
- io ifc/openings.rs `emit` writes, for EVERY opening, a void through the full wall thickness and then an IfcDoor for anything that is not a window (an `if` on Window, not an exhaustive match), filled in with IfcRelFillsElement: a new OpeningKind compiles straight into a phantom door.
- 3D pick (arch3d pick.rs PickHit {object, part}), engine pick3d `label_and_handle`, ui-egui pane3d `proxy_handle`/`surface_of`/`locate` and doc `part_handle` map owners from the part string alone; phase-18 materials that share a part with another owner (glass-wall -> glass, countertop -> counter, molding-*, backsplash) need the material-aware owner (p18-scene-plumbing item 8).

- (Checked in review round 3.) roomdetect bounds rooms with every wall that is not a divider (crates/arch/src/room.rs detect_rooms_at_level solves the poché of all of them), so a glass wall is a room edge (a corner shower enclosure becomes a room of its own) and a free-standing pony wall a hole ring. BRIEF VS CODE, DECIDED HERE:
(1) Room-type finishes are derived (see above); the template is code (`cadcraft_arch::room_finish::template`), the brief's "in the template" has no file format in this codebase. Wall faces and ceilings take Drywall/Plaster, flat finishes whose meshes are not split, so walls and ceilings look as before; floors show the template finish (wood for Other/Living/Dining/Entry, carpet for Bedroom/Closet/Family/OpenBelow, tile for Kitchen/Bath/Laundry/Utility, concrete for Garage/Porch, wood for Deck). Phase-13 check 02's "a model without finishes exports no images" clause is changed to a model without rooms (deliberate, stated in p18-room-finishes).
(2) Corner cabinets are derived from geometry (a cabinet holding the corner square where two perpendicular runs of its kind meet), with a stored preference `corner` (auto|none|blind|diagonal|lazySusan); auto = blind when the other run butts against its front, lazy susan when the other run starts further out (up to 1.2 m), filling the L to it.
(3) Fillers are cabinets with `filler: true`, width (0, 0.15], made only by cabinet.fill; they join counter runs like any base cabinet.
(4) Backsplash default material is unpainted `default:backsplash`; `backsplash {"material"}` writes a paint (tile in the demo). Countertops are unpainted `default:counter` like automatic counters; detaching copies the run's counter paint.
(5) Face appliances are face rows of kind appliance whose height is the appliance item's height; three built-in items (microwave drawer 0.381, wall oven 0.737, warming drawer 0.254, all 0.762 wide); a tall cabinet takes two, base and wall cabinets one.
(6) Countertop `elevation` (brief) is the TOP of the counter above the level floor (as `counterTop`); detached countertops keep top = run top + thickness.
(7) Edge moves keep the adjacent edges' directions (for the brief's rectangle that is +0.3·L exactly); sagitta > 0 bulges outward; the radius grip sets the sagitta of the minor arc.
(8) Molding lengths are the 2D lengths of the sweep paths along the wall faces (the profile's back line), runs broken at openings whose vertical extent overlaps the molding's band; crown follows the finished ceiling (flat rooms in p18-room-moldings; vaults, stacks, soffits, trays in p18-crown-wrap).
(9) Glass walls: type "Glass" (any case), 0.01 thick, default height 2.0, not exterior, layers [("glass", t)], no poché fill, material `glass-wall` (probe part `glass`, id w<n>). Pony walls: `wall.split` keeps the lower part as the wall's own type/thickness/joins and builds the upper part as a centred straight prism of its own thickness.
(10) Niches are OpeningKind::Niche made by a new `niche` command, cut through the wall in 3D and filled back behind the recess by a `niche-back:o<n>` block owned by the wall; refused unless wall thickness >= depth + 0.012 (1e-9 tolerance). A niche (and a frameless shower door) is cut at exactly width x height (cut_allowance 0, not today's 0.12 rough allowance), so the back block fills the whole recess area and nothing opens through the wall beside or above it.
